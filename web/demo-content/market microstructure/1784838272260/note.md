Risk aversion is commonly quantified through a utility function. Instead of maximizing pure profit, a market maker maximizes "expected utility", which applies to a mathematical penalty to uncertainy or variance
- Risk aversion coefficient (γ or α)
  - In Avellaneda-Stokikov, risk aversion dictates how much a market maker hates holding inventory
  - if γ is low (near 0): The market maker is "risk neutral". They only care about profit and are willing to hold large inventory positions to get it.
  - If γ is high: The market maker is "risk averse". They will aggresively move their prices to shed inventory, even if it means taking a lower profit or paying a spread.
